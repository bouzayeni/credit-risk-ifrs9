## 2. PD model: calibration, master scale and lifetime curves

| | |
|---|---|
| Purpose | 12-month point-in-time PD for each loan, rating grade, and lifetime PD for IFRS 9 |
| Version | `models/pd_calibration.json`, `processed/pd_term_structure.parquet` (Phase 4) |
| Calibration | Logistic fit of 12-month default on the frozen score, on the latest complete year (2017, 443,579 loans): intercept 16.344, slope -0.03304 (the points scale implies -0.03466) |
| Master scale | R1–R10 with fixed PD bounds; grade PD = observed rate of the calibration year, from 1.02% (R1) to 20.35% (R10) |
| Lifetime PD | Discrete-time competing risks (default vs repayment) by rating and term, 3-month recognition lag, anchored to the grade PD; 5 of 20 rating-term curves borrow the term's shape in thin months |
| Report | [phase4_pd.md](phase4_pd.md) |

**Validation** (calibrate on one year, predict the next):

| Design | Calibrated on | 2017 predicted | 2017 observed | Grades failing Jeffreys (5%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First attempt | 2015–2016 | 5.60% | 6.13% | 6 of 10 |
| Validated design | 2016 | 6.14% | 6.13% | 3 of 10 |

The design was changed after the first 2017 result; both are reported.

**Lifetime back-test** (whole chain from origination, Phase 6):
2014, 36 months: lifetime PD 16.9% predicted vs 13.7% actual; 2015, 36 months: lifetime PD 16.7% predicted vs 14.9% actual; 2013, 60 months: lifetime PD 29.5% predicted vs 25.1% actual.
The over-prediction comes mostly from the first year, where the 2017 level is applied to
lower-risk vintages.

**Use and limits.** Point-in-time on 2017 loans. Older vintages defaulted less, and 2018
loans may default more (6-month defaults of 2018Q2 loans at 1.15× the curve, Phase 7):
recalibrate when 2018 12-month outcomes are complete.

## 3. LGD model

| | |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Share of exposure lost after default, for IFRS 9 |
| Version | `models/lgd.json` (Phase 5) |
| Data | 174,565 of 268,537 charged-off loans, those defaulted at least 18 months before the snapshot (complete recovery workout) |
| Method | LGD = 1 − net recovery rate × discount factor; net recovery = recoveries − collection fees, over principal at default; recoveries discounted at the loan rate with a timing profile inferred from the cross-section (mean recovery month 9.3); segment by rating, EAD-weighted |
| Result | 90.9% pooled (89.6% undiscounted); 21.6% of defaults recover nothing; by rating 89.0% (R1) to 91.7% (R10); pooled value used for R1 (under 1,000 workouts) |
| Report | [phase5_lgd_ead.md](phase5_lgd_ead.md) |

**Use and limits.** LGD barely varies by rating, term or default year. Debt settlement does
matter (LGD 74.9% with a settlement, 92.6% without), but it is agreed after default, so it cannot
segment future losses. Recovery dates are not in the data. No cure is modelled for loans
31+ days late, which is conservative.

## 5. IFRS 9 expected credit loss

| | |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Loss allowance for the open book at 2019-03-31 |
| Staging | Stage 3: 31+ days past due or default. Stage 2: 1–30 days past due, hardship plan, or current 12-month PD at least 2× origination (current PD = scorecard re-applied with March 2019 FICO). Stage 1: the rest |
| Engine | Monthly marginal PD (Phase 4 curves from the current month on book) × LGD × contractual EAD × discount at the loan rate; 12 months for stage 1, remaining life for stage 2, LGD × balance for stage 3 |
| Delinquent loans | Roll rates to 31+ days from steady-state bucket stocks: grace period 42.9%, 16–30 days 82.8% |
| Scenarios | Single-factor (Vasicek) shifts of the 12-month PD for 24 months; rho 3% (estimated 1.1% on a recession-free period, floored at the Basel retail minimum); upside / base / downside weighted 30% / 50% / 20% |
| Report | [phase6_ecl.md](phase6_ecl.md) |
