Power BI, in `processed/monitoring.parquet`.

2015Q1 to 2018Q4, as a bank's model monitoring would run them. Re-scoring all loans from
`models/scorecard.json` reproduces the Phase 3 scores exactly (0 mismatches),
so the saved model is complete enough to deploy.

## What the monitoring shows

1. **Calibration drift, not population drift, broke the original scorecard PD.** The
   scorecard's own PD (Phase 3 points scale) under-predicted 12-month defaults from
   2015Q3 onwards (observed / predicted 1.20, red), peaking at
   1.49 in 2016Q3. Meanwhile the score distribution barely moved (PSI under 0.05
   through 2017Q3), and the mean score even rose (578.0 in 2015Q1, 584.9 in 2018Q4). Applicants
   looked the same or better on paper but defaulted more, so the link from score to PD had
   changed. This is the trigger for the Phase 4 recalibration, which brings 2016–2017 back to
   green. It over-predicts 2015 (observed / predicted 0.77–0.89), a lower-risk year.
2. **The population shifted from 2017Q4.** FICO (CSI 0.046 in 2017Q3, 0.144 in
   2017Q4) and available credit on bank cards (`bc_open_to_buy`, up to 0.278) moved first, then
   recent inquiries and verification status in 2018. The score PSI reached amber in 2018Q4
   (0.102). The shift is towards safer scores: the bottom score band of the 2013–2014
   reference held 10.8% of loans, and only 5.3% in 2018Q4. That is
   consistent with a tightening of credit policy (the lower FICO quartile rose from 672 to 682
   in 2017Q4).
3. **Discrimination is weakening but still green.** The Gini fell from 0.36–0.39 in 2015 to
   0.322 at its lowest (2016Q4, standard error 0.007), against an amber line of
   0.317 (90% of the development Gini, 0.352). The 6-month Gini, the only one available for
   2018 loans, is 0.31–0.33 for 2018Q1–Q2, no lower than in 2017
   (a different horizon, so it has no traffic light).
4. **Early warning for 2018 loans.** Defaults within 6 months are compared with the Phase 4
   curves (which already include the 2017 calibration). 2018Q2 loans default
   1.15× the expected rate (🟡), despite their better scores. If 12-month
   outcomes confirm it, the production PD will need recalibrating to 2018.

**Recommended actions:** (1) keep the Phase 4 recalibration; (2) watch 2018 vintages
monthly, and recalibrate if the early-default ratio stays above 1.1; (3) plan a redevelopment
on 2016–2018 data: the population has shifted, discrimination is drifting towards amber, and
the original development data includes years in which four of the twelve features were not
yet reported.

**Reference population.** Stability is measured against 2013–2014 loans (370,443),
the development years in which every scorecard field is reported. Four bureau fields
(`mo_sin_old_rev_tl_op`, `acc_open_past_24mths`, `mo_sin_old_il_acct`, `bc_open_to_buy`) start in
2012. Against the full 2007–2014 development sample, 14.6% of loans sit in their
MISSING bin, so every later quarter would show a false CSI above 0.25 for those features. The
first run of this report did exactly that.

## Thresholds

| Metric | Green | Amber | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| PSI / CSI | below 0.10 | 0.10–0.25 | 0.25 or more |
| Gini vs development (0.352) | at least 90% | 80%–90% | below 80% |
| Observed / predicted default rate | within ±10%, or not significant | ±10%–20% and significant | beyond ±20% and significant |

Significance: two-sided z-test at 5%, with loans treated as independent. Correlated
defaults make that overstate significance, which is why a status also needs a material gap.
The Gini standard error is Hanley–McNeil (1982).

## Limitations

- Loans are monitored by issue quarter, so 12-month outcomes lag by 15 months. The
  6-month indicator shortens that to 9 months, but on a different horizon.
- There are no rejected applications. PSI covers booked loans only, so a change in
  Lending Club's approval policy shows up as population drift.
- The thresholds are industry conventions, not statistically derived.
