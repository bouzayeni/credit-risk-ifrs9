Lending Club loans are unsecured, high-yield consumer loans, so coverage is far above a bank
mortgage book: 12-month expected loss on stage 1 alone is about one year of defaults
(mean forward 12-month PD 7.7% in the base scenario) times an LGD of about 91%.
The 12-month PD of a seasoned loan is above its first-year PD (Phase 4 grade PDs), because
the monthly default rate of surviving loans is highest in their second year on book
(about 1.6–1.7× the first year's for 36-month loans in the middle grades).

## Method

ECL = Σ over future months of marginal PD × LGD × EAD × discount factor, per loan:

- **PD**: the Phase 4 lifetime curves (default and repayment hazards by rating and term,
  anchored to the point-in-time grade PD), started at the loan's current month on book, so
  they are conditional on the loan being alive today. Rating = *current* rating (below).
- **LGD**: Phase 5, by current rating, discounted to the default date.
- **EAD**: Phase 5, contractual balance in each future month.
- **Discounting**: at the loan's contract rate (the effective interest rate, ignoring fees).
- **Stage 1**: next 12 months; **stage 2**: remaining contractual life; **stage 3**: PD = 1,
  ECL = LGD × gross carrying amount.
- Probability-weighted over three scenarios.

## Staging

**Current rating.** The application scorecard is re-applied with the borrower's FICO as of
March 2019 (`last_fico`, refreshed monthly by Lending Club for 99.85% of open loans) instead of
the FICO at application; the other features stay as at origination. Below the scorecard's
lowest FICO bin, the FICO WoE is extrapolated along the line through the bin WoEs
(0.0123 WoE per FICO point), floored at FICO 500.

**Rules** (first match wins):

| Stage | Rule |
|---|---|
| 3 | 31+ days past due or in default (the Phase 1 default definition) |
| 2 | 1–30 days past due (grace period or 16–30 days late), on a hardship plan, or current 12-month PD at least 2× the PD at origination |
| 1 | all other loans |

The PD test uses 12-month PDs as a proxy for lifetime PDs (IFRS 9 B5.5.13). Both PDs come
from the same calibration, so the ratio measures the change in the borrower, not in the economy.
IFRS 9's 30-days-past-due backstop is covered, since every loan 1–30 days late is already in stage 2.

**Stage 2 drivers** (39,018 loans; a loan can meet several rules): delinquent 12,643;
PD test 28,247 (26,371 on the PD test alone); hardship plan 103 (not
31+ days late). Most hardship-plan loans are already 31+ days late and sit in stage 3.

## Delinquent loans

A loan 1–30 days late is far riskier than its rating says, but the data is a single snapshot
with no payment history to measure that. Roll rates are inferred from the stock of loans in
each delinquency bucket, assuming a steady state: the monthly flow into a bucket = its stock /
months spent there (½ month for the grace period and for 16–30 days, 3 months for 31–120
days), and every loan reaching 31+ days passed through the earlier buckets.

| Bucket | Probability of reaching 31+ days late |
|---|---|
| In Grace Period (1–15 days) | 42.9% |
| Late (16–30 days) | 82.8% |

These loans default next month with that probability (ECL = probability × LGD × balance),
and otherwise follow their rating's lifetime curve. Cures shorten the stay in every bucket,
so these estimates can be off in either direction. Without them, ECL would be
$860.2M (-6.6%).

## Forward-looking scenarios

**Why not a macro regression.** From 2010 to 2017, US unemployment fell while Lending Club's
default rates rose, as it loosened its credit policy. A regression of default rates on
unemployment would get the wrong sign. Scenarios are instead built on a single-factor
(Vasicek) model, where one systematic factor moves every grade's default rate:

    probit(PD_scenario) = probit(PD_base) + k × sqrt(rho / (1 − rho))

**Systematic risk (rho).** It's measured from the year-to-year swings of the 12-month default rate
within each rating grade (issue years 2010–2017, cells with at least 500 loans), after
removing binomial noise:

| Grade | Issue years | Variance of probit default rate | Of which sampling noise | Systematic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | 6 | 0.0193 | 0.0134 | 0.0059 |
| R2 | 6 | 0.0127 | 0.0016 | 0.0112 |
| R3 | 8 | 0.0422 | 0.0042 | 0.0380 |
| R4 | 8 | 0.0162 | 0.0011 | 0.0151 |
| R5 | 8 | 0.0103 | 0.0006 | 0.0097 |
| R6 | 8 | 0.0058 | 0.0004 | 0.0054 |
| R7 | 8 | 0.0067 | 0.0005 | 0.0062 |
| R8 | 8 | 0.0060 | 0.0007 | 0.0053 |
| R9 | 7 | 0.0093 | 0.0008 | 0.0085 |
| R10 | 6 | 0.0080 | 0.0010 | 0.0070 |

Estimated rho = 1.14%. That is below 3%, the lower end of the Basel
IRB range for other retail exposures, because 2010–2017 had no recession. The model therefore
uses rho = 3% (estimated value in the sensitivities).

**Scenarios.** The shift k is in standard deviations of the systematic factor. It applies to
default hazards for the first 24 months, after which the base curve resumes. The
weights and shifts are judgement, like the management scenarios they stand for. LGD is held
constant (Phase 5 found only small differences between default years).

The probability-weighted ECL is 4.7% above the base scenario. Part of this is the
scenario set leaning adverse (average shift +0.1 sd). The rest is non-linearity: PDs
rise faster in a downturn than they fall in an upturn (a 2 sd downside adds
$449.8M, more than twice what the 1 sd upside takes off,
$162.5M). That is why IFRS 9 requires several scenarios rather
than one central case.

## Back-test of the lifetime chain on matured vintages

The full chain (origination rating → lifetime PD curve → EAD schedule → LGD) is run from
month 0 for vintages that have fully matured. Loss is undiscounted and uses undiscounted LGD,
like the realised figure: principal at default minus net recoveries, over amount funded.
Base scenario.

| Vintage | Loans | 12m PD predicted / actual | Lifetime PD predicted / actual | Lifetime loss predicted / actual | Ratio (loss) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014, 36 months | 162,570 | 6.2% / 4.3% | 16.9% / 13.7% | 8.9% / 6.7% | 1.33 |
| 2015, 36 months | 283,173 | 6.1% / 4.9% | 16.7% / 14.9% | 8.7% / 7.6% | 1.15 |
| 2013, 60 months | 34,392 | 8.4% / 6.0% | 29.5% / 25.1% | 19.1% / 15.4% | 1.23 |

The chain over-predicts every vintage, and most of the gap is already in the first year
(12-month PD). That gap is the point-in-time calibration on 2017 loans: older vintages
defaulted less than 2017 loans in the same grade (Phase 4: 2016–2017 loans defaulted 30–50%
more than 2013–2014 loans). Over the full life the PD over-prediction is smaller than in
year one, and LGD and EAD add 3–8% on top of the PD gap. For the March 2019 book, the
2017 level is the relevant one, but the back-test shows how much the allowance depends on it.

## Sensitivities

| Case | Stage 2 loans | ECL | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| SICR at 1.5x origination PD | 91,651 | $935.8M | +1.6% |
| SICR at 2x origination PD | 39,018 | $920.9M | +0.0% |
| SICR at 3x origination PD | 16,481 | $915.9M | -0.5% |
| No SICR: every performing loan on 12-month ECL | 0 | $913.1M | -0.8% |
| Every performing loan on lifetime ECL | 886,423 | $1,186.1M | +28.8% |
| No FICO extrapolation below the scorecard's lowest bin | 12,647 | $897.6M | -2.5% |
| Delinquent loans on the rating curve only (no roll rates) | 39,018 | $860.2M | -6.6% |
| Systematic risk as estimated (rho 1.1% instead of 3%) | 39,018 | $899.3M | -2.3% |

- **Staging matters less than in a bank book.** Moving every performing loan from 12-month to
  lifetime ECL adds $273.0M, but the actual SICR rules move only
  $7.8M of it. Most performing loans are not deteriorating, and remaining
  lives are short (median 26 months).
- **The biggest single assumption is the delinquent-loan roll rates** (-6.6% without
  them). The FICO extrapolation and the systematic risk floor each move ECL by 2–3%.

## Limitations

- **Current rating** updates FICO only; other application features (DTI, income, inquiries)
  are as at origination, and the application scorecard's FICO weight probably understates how
  predictive a FICO *fall* is.
- **SICR and roll rates can't be back-tested** with a single snapshot.
- **No cure** is assumed for stage 3 loans (31+ days late), and the stage 3 LGD comes from
  charged-off loans, so stage 3 is conservative.
- **Systematic risk** is floored at a regulatory value, and the scenario weights are judgement.
- **The effective interest rate** ignores origination fees, and EAD ignores accrued interest.
