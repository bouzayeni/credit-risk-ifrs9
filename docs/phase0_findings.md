# Phase 0: dataset inspection and design decisions

Source: `accepted_2007_to_2018Q4.csv.gz` from the Kaggle dataset
`wordsforthewise/lending-club` (CC0). SHA-256 in `data/raw/_manifest.json`. Full column
profile: [phase0_profile.json](phase0_profile.json), generated by
`python -m credit_risk.data.profile_raw`.

## What is in the file

- **2,260,668 loans**, 151 columns, issued 2007-06 to 2018-12. The file also has 33 footer
  lines ("Total amount funded in policy code 1: ...") with no loan status; the profiler drops
  them.
- **Snapshot, not history.** Each loan has one row with its status at the end of 2018 plus
  cumulative amounts (`total_rec_prncp`, `recoveries`, ...) and dates (`last_pymnt_d`). There
  is no monthly payment history, so days past due over time cannot be rebuilt.
- **Term:** 36 months 1,609,754 (71%), 60 months 650,914 (29%).
- **Grade:** A 433k, B 664k, C 650k, D 324k, E 136k, F 42k, G 12k.
- **Volume by year** grows from 603 loans (2007) to 495,242 (2018).

## Findings that shape the design

1. **Recent vintages are unfinished.** 2007–2012 loans are all closed; from 2014 on, more and
   more are still `Current` (86% of 2018 loans). Counting only closed loans would make recent
   vintages look safer or riskier depending on which loans close first (early repayers close
   early, defaulters close at about 5 months past due). The target therefore needs a **fixed
   outcome window** measured from issue date, not the final status.
2. **A 12-month default flag is possible without payment history.** Lending Club charges off
   at about 150 days past due, and `last_pymnt_d` gives the last payment. For defaulted loans,
   the first missed payment is about one month after `last_pymnt_d`, which dates the default
   event. Every loan issued up to 2017-12 has a full 12 months of observation before the
   snapshot. 2,313 charged-off loans have no `last_pymnt_d` (defaulted before any payment):
   their default date is the first due date.
3. **Default definition** (to be fixed in Phase 1): Charged Off, Default and Late (31–120) are
   candidates. The regulatory definition is 90+ days past due; the 31–120 bucket straddles it,
   so it cannot be split exactly. Sensitivity of the default rate to this choice will be shown.
4. **Exclusions:** 2,749 "Does not meet the credit policy" loans (2007–2010, old policy).
5. **Leakage.** Many columns are only known after origination and must never enter the
   application scorecard: `out_prncp*`, `total_pymnt*`, `total_rec_*`, `recoveries`,
   `collection_recovery_fee`, `last_pymnt_*`, `next_pymnt_d`, `last_credit_pull_d`,
   `last_fico_range_*`, `hardship_*`, `settlement_*`, `debt_settlement_flag*`, `pymnt_plan`.
   They are still useful later: recoveries for LGD, repaid principal for EAD, and the change
   in FICO since origination as a significant-increase-in-credit-risk signal for IFRS 9
   staging.
6. **Grade and interest rate are Lending Club's own risk score.** Using them as inputs would
   just copy LC's model. The scorecard is built **without** `grade`, `sub_grade` and
   `int_rate`, and then compared with grade as a benchmark.
7. **Fields added over time.** 38% of loans lack `open_acc_6m`, `il_util`, `inq_fi` and
   similar fields; they are filled from 2016-01 onwards. Missing here means "not collected
   yet", not "applicant has none", so these fields need care in out-of-time validation.
   Secondary-applicant fields (`sec_app_*`, about 95% empty) apply to the 120,710 joint
   applications only.
8. **Anomalies to handle in cleaning:** `annual_inc` up to 110M (583 loans above 1M) and 1,667
   at 0; 2,563 loans with `dti` below 0 or above 100; `member_id` is always empty; `url`,
   `desc`, `emp_title` and `title` are free text.
9. **ECL portfolio.** At the 2018-12-31 reporting date, the open book is the 878,317 Current
   loans plus 34,252 in grace or late. Their status gives a days-past-due view only in coarse
   buckets, which is enough for the 30 days past due backstop (stage 2) and for stage 3.

## Practical constraints

- The development PC has 7.8 GB RAM. The raw file is 1.6 GB uncompressed; it is read in
  chunks and converted once to a typed Parquet file with only the needed columns (Phase 1).
- Data and the Python environment live on `K:` (the system drive is almost full).
